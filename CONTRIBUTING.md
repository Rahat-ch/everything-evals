# Contributing

Suggestions are welcome. There are two ways to add something:
- **Open an issue** with the "Suggest a resource" template. You don't need to edit any files.
- **Open a pull request** that adds the entry to `resources.md` yourself.

Broken links and out-of-date details (someone changed jobs, a page moved) are just as useful. Use the "Broken link or outdated info" template.

## What gets in

A resource is added only if all of these are true:

1. **It's by someone who works in AI or is a researcher.** That means an AI or ML engineer or scientist, someone at an AI lab or company, an academic researcher, or a government or standards research body. Influencers, marketers and general content channels don't qualify, even when the content is good.
2. **It's free.** It has to be free to read or watch with no paywall. A free part of a paid course is fine if the entry says what's free.
3. **It teaches something about evals.** That covers methodology, error analysis, LLM-as-judge, statistics, benchmarks, agents, human labelling, tools, or safety evals.
4. **It isn't a sales pitch.** Educational posts from companies are fine. Product marketing isn't.
5. **The link works.** Give the final URL, after any redirect.

## Entry format

Add the entry to the matching section of `resources.md`, in this format:

```
- **[Title](link)** · Author(s), org · *why they're credible: one line on what they've built, run or published* · format (blog, paper, docs, course, talk, repo) · rough length · date (YYYY-MM or YYYY-MM-DD)
```

The "why credible" line must be checkable. Link or name the source for it: their own site, their employer or lab page, or the paper's author list.

Keep the writing plain: short sentences and no hype words.
